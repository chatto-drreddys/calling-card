## MODULE 2 — Leadership in the Moment (Days 2–3)

### Darwin adage
> "It is not the strongest of the species that survive, nor the most intelligent, but the one most **responsive to change**."
Key words the exam may test: **responsive, adaptable, flexible, adjust**. Survival = adaptability, not strength/intelligence.

### Paradigm (high-yield definition)
- **Paradigm** = a set of **assumptions, concepts, values, and practices** that constitutes a way of viewing reality. It **determines behavior**; described as universal, timeless, inarguable, and it controls the consequences of our choices.
- **Paradigm can be POSITIVE or NEGATIVE.** A **principle is ALWAYS positive.** (Common trap.)
- (Stephen Covey) Paradigms — correct or incorrect — are the **source of our attitudes, behaviors, and ultimately our relationships** with others.
- Paradigms often form from subconscious/childhood experiences and upbringing.

### Victim vs Victor mentality
| Victim paradigm | Victor paradigm |
|---|---|
| Makes excuses, "woe is me" | "Despite circumstances, I'll rise to my greater self" |
| Blames others, points fingers | Embraces challenges as growth |
| Crushed by circumstances | "Circumstances are meant to create me, not crush me" |

### The Change Chart (sequence matters)
**Change** (circumstance / issue / shortcoming / any negative situation) → triggers your **Paradigm & Principles** → produces your **Behavior / Reaction**.

- **Negative path (Victim cycle):** negative paradigm → negative behavior → **fear / threat → fight-or-flight** (fight = aggressive, moody, blaming; flight = avoidance) → piles up **Resentment, Resistance, Revenge** (and Regret) → repeats as a cycle.
- **Break the cycle → Victor cycle:** adjust your paradigm/principles → seek advice (trusted peers, workmates, managers, or spiritual guidance) → **be proactive** → leads to **Choice, Opportunities, Creativity/Solutions** → ready for the next challenge without resentment.
- Quote (Beth Mende Connie): *"Life is a book and you are its author. You determine its plot and pace; only you turn its pages."*

### Habit 1 — Be Proactive
- Definition: **finding your mission in life and charting your own destiny** using the 4 human endowments: **Self-awareness, Creativity (imagination), Conscience, Will (willpower).**
- "Decide what you want, take responsibility for your decisions; face reality, accept what you cannot control, and choose a positive response."

**Reactive vs Proactive (memorize both columns):**
| Reactive | Proactive |
|---|---|
| Reacts | Responds |
| Blames; affected by physical environment | Takes responsibility; copes with & controls environment |
| Feels out of control | Chooses own behavior |
| Acts on **feelings** / conditions | Acts on **values** / conscious choice |
| Language: **"I can't, I must, if only"** | Language: **"I can, I will, I prefer"** |

**3 characteristics from the proactive video:**
1. Proactive people **don't blame**; reactive people blame circumstances, conditions, and conditioning.
2. Proactive behavior = product of **conscious choice based on values**; reactive = product of **conditions based on feelings**.
3. Proactive people are still influenced by external stimulus but their **response is a value-based choice**; reactive people build their lives around others' emotions.

**3 ways to be more proactive:** ① **Predict and prevent** (anticipate problems) ② **Plan** (daily agendas / to-do lists) ③ **Clear goals & vision** — set milestones, consult someone experienced, surround yourself with passionate people.

### MASTER checklist (handling adversity proactively)
**M**easure (how important is it, scale 1–5) → **A**ssess (your initial reaction) → **S**ee (the stimulus–response model) → **T**hink (a proactive response) → **E**ngage (use the chosen response **or exit**) → **R**eview (how proactive you were).
- It's OK to *react* internally; just **don't act on the reaction** — choose your response.

### Cockroach story (key distinction — high-yield)
- It's not the problem (cockroach) but your **inability to handle the disturbance** that disturbs you.
- **Reactions are instinctive → chaos. Responses are intellectual → control.**
- "More than the problem, the **reaction** to the problem matters." The women **reacted**; the waiter **responded**. → **Respond, don't react.**

### Habit 7 — Sharpen the Saw
- = **constantly renewing yourself in 4 basic dimensions**: **Physical, Social/Emotional, Mental, Spiritual.** (Memorize all 4.)
- Renewal must be **balanced** — neglecting one dimension negatively impacts the rest.
- "We are the **instruments of our own performance**" — investing in yourself is the single most powerful investment.

**Covey's 4-dimension challenge:**
| Dimension | Assume… | Now… |
|---|---|---|
| Body | you've had a heart attack | diet & exercise accordingly |
| Mind | the half-life of your profession is 2 years | prepare/keep learning accordingly |
| Heart | everything you say about others, they overhear | speak accordingly |
| Spirit | a quarterly one-on-one with your creator | live accordingly |

- **Productivity rule:** take a **5–10 minute break every hour**; don't work to exhaustion (dull-saw vs sharp-saw example — sharpening saves time and reduces errors).
- **4 ways to simplify life:** schedule downtime daily; plan to pay off credit; leave the TV off one night a week; don't be a slave to your phone (+ be patient with yourself).

### 5 Steps to Inner Renewal (memorize all 5)
**Remember** (capture a dream, make it real) → **Create** (make something from something simple) → **Absorb** (savor a gift that's yours — music, a flower) → **Want** (a want) → **Need** (deeper than a want; must be addressed at all costs).
- Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs was referenced here (want vs need).

**Opening adage (John Kinsey Adams):**

**Core definition:** Moving from personal effectiveness → **greatness through legacy**.

**Key distinction (exam trap):**

### Habit 5 — Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood
> "Most people do not listen with the intent to understand; they listen with the intent to reply." — Stephen Covey

**Why it matters:** You cannot lead, manage, or build relationships if you don't understand the other person first.

#### 5 Levels of Listening (low → high) — MEMORIZE SEQUENCE
1. **Ignoring** — not listening at all
2. **Pretending** — "Yeah... uh huh..."
3. **Selective** — hearing only parts that interest you
4. **Attentive** — paying attention, focusing on words
5. **Empathic** — listening with intent to understand (feeling, meaning, not just words)

*Empathic = the goal. This is Habit 5.*

#### 4 Autobiographical Responses (why we fail to listen — EXAM TRAP)
When we listen to reply instead of to understand, we fall into these traps:
1. **Evaluate** — agree or disagree
2. **Probe** — ask from our own frame of reference
3. **Advise** — give counsel based on our own experience
4. **Interpret** — explain motives based on our own experience

*All 4 are self-referential. They make the conversation about YOU, not the other person.*

#### Empathic Listening in Practice
- Reflect feeling: "It sounds like you're frustrated that..."
- Reflect content: "So what you're saying is..."
- Both: "It sounds like you feel [emotion] because [reason]..."
- **Do NOT:** jump to solutions, compare to your own experience, or finish their sentences

### The 13 Behaviors of Trusted People (Stephen M.R. Covey — Speed of Trust)
**(Memorize all 13 — high-yield exam list)**

1. **Talk Straight** — be honest, tell the truth, use clear language; no spin
2. **Demonstrate Respect** — genuinely care for others; show it in actions
3. **Create Transparency** — be open, no hidden agendas, don't hide information
4. **Right Wrongs** — apologize quickly, make restitution, don't cover up
5. **Show Loyalty** — give credit to others; speak about people as if they're present; true loyalty = honest, not blind
6. **Deliver Results** — establish a track record; don't just talk about results
7. **Get Better** — continuously improve; learn from experience; don't stagnate
8. **Confront Reality** — address tough stuff directly; don't skirt difficult issues
9. **Clarify Expectations** — create shared understanding upfront; don't assume
10. **Practice Accountability** — take ownership of your actions; admit mistakes; don't blame others
11. **Listen First** — listen before you speak; understand before prescribing ("talking earns attention; listening earns trust")
12. **Keep Commitments** — say what you'll do, do what you say; small promises compound
13. **Extend Trust** — give trust to those who have earned it; conditionally to those earning it

**Key principle:** *"How you behave in one place gets talked about in other places. By behaving in ways that build trust with one, you build trust with many."* (ripple effect)

**Trust = integrity.** Brian Tracy adage:
> "The glue that holds all relationships together, including the relationship between the leader and the lead, is trust. And trust is based on integrity."

### Communicating for Success

**Communication breakdown (exam numbers — 10/30/60):**
- **Words = 10%** of communication
- **Sounds (tone, pace) = 30%**
- **Body language = 60%**

*Implication: You can say the right words and still be misunderstood if tone and body language contradict them.*

**4 principles of effective communication:**
1. **Communicate with clarity** — clear vocabulary, verbal delivery; especially critical across global/multicultural teams
2. **Listen** — prepare to understand, not to respond; listen to ideas, thoughts, and feelings; avoid multitasking; watch non-verbal behavior; check for understanding
3. **Group communication** — be inclusive; create agenda; use open-ended questions; bring in quieter voices; avoid reactions that signal judgment
4. **Never assume** — clarify, ask, document what was agreed on (minutes of meeting, AI transcripts)

**Bonus: Sense of humor** — invaluable tool for building rapport; use appropriately; makes the team's day; warms up communication

**Maintaining composure** — communicating without frustration when resisted or blocked; watch your tone; stay composed especially in stressful situations

### Habit 5 — Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood

**Core insight:** *"We listen to reply, not to understand."* — Stephen Covey

**4 Autobiographical Responses (what to AVOID — exam trap, all 4 are wrong):**
1. **Probing** — asking questions from your own frame of reference
2. **Evaluating** — agreeing or disagreeing (judging)
3. **Advising** — giving counsel based on your own experience
4. **Interpreting** — explaining motives/behavior based on your own experience

*All 4 are self-referential. You put yourself at the center instead of the other person.*

**The answer: Empathic Listening**
- Step into the other person's shoes
- Understand their emotions, frame of reference, intent
- *"Understanding does not mean agreement."* You can understand without agreeing.
- The empathic response: **"What can I do to help you in this situation?"**
  - Doesn't evaluate, probe, advise, or interpret
  - Keeps you in the other person's frame of reference
  - Protects you from getting blamed for unsolicited advice

**Why listening is rarely taught:**
- 4 ways of communication: reading, writing, speaking, **listening**
- Schools train reading, writing, speaking rigorously — **listening is ignored altogether**
- Hence it is the most underdeveloped communication skill

**Diagnose before prescribing:** A doctor doesn't prescribe without diagnosing. An engineer studies stresses before designing. A leader listens before prescribing a solution.

### Simon Sinek — Trusting Teams (key video)

**Four Seasons story (Noah the barista):**
- Noah said *"I love my job"* (not just "like" — love = emotional connection)
- Why? Managers walked past and asked how he was doing, whether he needed anything — ANY manager, not just his own
- At Caesar's Palace (same Noah): kept his head below the radar, collected his paycheck
- *Same person. Profoundly different experience. The difference = the leadership environment.*

**The correct question:**
- ❌ Wrong: *"How do we get the most out of our people?"* (treats people like resources to squeeze)
- ✅ Right: *"How do we create an environment in which our people can work at their natural best?"*

**Without trusting teams, people show up every day:**
- **Lying** (hiding mistakes)
- **Hiding** (won't admit they were promoted beyond their capability)
- **Faking** (performing rather than contributing)

**With trusting teams (Safe Space):**
- People say "I made a mistake" without fear of humiliation
- People admit "I don't know how to do this, I need training"
- People say "I'm struggling at home and it's affecting my work"
- People ask for help — with confidence their team will rush to support them

**The Two S's (Eleanor's closing question — exam-tested):**
**Safe Space** — what a good and effective leader must provide to their team.

**United Airlines example (what happens without trust):**
- A passenger was dragged off a plane with broken nose, teeth, concussion
- Every crew member knew it was wrong — but none spoke up
- Why? They *"feared getting in trouble more than doing the right thing"*
- They did not work on trusting teams. This was a slow build, not an anomaly.

**Virgin Atlantic vs United Airlines:**
- Virgin: people feel safe, trusted to do the job they were trained for → customer, company, and staff all benefit
- United: people fear punishment → customer, company, and staff all suffer

**Leadership is a lifestyle (key exam quote):**
> "Leadership is not about being in charge. Leadership is about taking care of those in your charge."
> "Leaders are not responsible for the results. They're responsible for the people who are responsible for the results."

Building trusting teams = going to the gym. You can't do it in one 9-hour session. You commit 20 minutes every day. 100% it works. No formula. A process. A lifestyle.

### Deep Customer Understanding

**Definition:** Going beyond surface/factual/quantitative understanding to relational, qualitative, extended understanding.

> "Remembering pieces of factual information alone is not deep understanding. To have deep understanding is to be able to put the pieces together and to use such understanding to do things, solve problems, create new ideas."

**3 Dimensions of Deep Customer Understanding (exam — know all 3):**
1. **Multiple stakeholders** — not just the end customer but the customer's customer; payers, users, maintainers, regulators (e.g., healthcare: patient + provider + payer + hospital)

**Surface vs Deep (exam — know the contrast):**
| Surface | Deep |
|---|---|
| "Client wants a dashboard by Friday" | Understand VP's needs vs daily user needs vs IT maintenance needs |
| "What features do you want?" | "What do you primarily use it for? Who uses the output? What did the last system get wrong?" |
| Functional only | Functional + Social + Emotional |
| Quantitative/factual | Relational, qualitative, extended |
| Solve the task | Serve the person, relationships, and emotional stakes |

**Deep understanding is relational.** It's more than what the customer needs — it's about how else you can help, what perks/value can be added, and building loyalty through extended service.

### CPR for Crucial Conversations

**CPR = Content → Pattern → Relationship** (in that sequence — exam: know all 3 and when to use each)

| Level | When to use | What it sounds like | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | Behavior NOT repetitive — first time | "Why was the report late this week?" | First verbal warning; address the specific issue |
| **Pattern** | Behavior IS repetitive — happening again | "We've talked about reports being on time and nothing seems to change. I'm wondering why." | Final warning; address the recurring pattern |
| **Relationship** | Trust is low, doubt is high — pattern conversation had no impact | "I'm starting to doubt that you actually want to fix the situation." | Coaching conversation; address the relationship |

**Key principle:** Address the **RIGHT** issue. Change up the conversation type if the current one isn't working. CPR comes BEFORE the Performance Improvement Plan (last resort).

**Eleanor's definition:**
- Content = first verbal warning
- Pattern = final warning
- Relationship = coaching conversation

## MODULE 5 — Creative Thinking (Day 6)

### Sun Tzu adage — Creativity (Eleanor's #1 adage of the entire 9-day program)
> "There are not more than five musical notes, yet the combinations of these five give rise to more melodies than can ever be heard. There are not more than five primary colors, yet in combination they produce more hues than can ever been seen. There are not more than five cardinal tastes, yet combinations of them yield more flavors than can ever be tasted."

**The answer (hangman clue: C _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ Y):** **CREATIVITY**

> "With creativity, there are always limitless possibilities."


### Three Branches of Creative Thinking
1. **Creative Mindset** — thinking outside the box, challenging assumptions
2. **Thinking Outside the Box** — lateral thinking, reframing problems
3. **Creative Ideas Generation** — deliberate ideation techniques

### Myths About Creativity (True/False — EXAM TESTED)
- ❌ Creativity is only for "creative types" / artists → **FALSE** (everyone can develop it)
- ❌ Creativity = accident/spontaneity only → **FALSE** (can be deliberate & structured)
- ❌ Creative people are born, not made → **FALSE** (skill that atrophies without practice)
- ✅ Creativity requires both divergent AND convergent thinking
- ✅ Constraints can enhance creativity (not hinder it)

### Deliberate vs Accidental Creativity
- **Deliberate** — planned, structured, documented, iterative (warm-up → ideate → prototype → test)
- **Accidental** — spontaneous, unplanned, serendipitous
- **Best leaders use BOTH** — deliberate practice warms up the brain for accidental insights

### Five-Point Platform for Raising Productivity via Creativity
1. **Challenge assumptions** — "What if the opposite were true?"
2. **Reframe the problem** — change the question to change the solution space
3. **Cross-pollinate** — borrow ideas from unrelated domains
4. **Prototype fast** — build cheap, test early, learn faster
5. **Iterate deliberately** — document, review, improve in cycles

### Creativity Warm-ups (Practiced in Lecture)
- **Count the F's** — perception/attention exercise (answer: 10 F's in "Finish f...")
- **Four Pics One Word** — pattern recognition, lateral thinking
- **Daily 10-minute puzzles/lateral-thinking prompts** — keeps brain flexible
- **Weekly 30-minute ideation sessions** — structured brainstorming

## Management vs. Leadership

For your exam, pay close attention to the distinctions between these two roles:

* **Management:** Focuses heavily on the control and regulation of resources, organizing processes, and ensuring that tasks are completed efficiently.


* **Leadership:** Focuses on handling the tangible and intangible needs of people, setting a vision, inspiring team members, and building trust.

## Tuckman's Stages of Team Development

Exam questions will likely ask you to identify a team's stage based on their current behaviors. Memorize these key characteristics:

* **Forming:** Team members are polite, unfamiliar with each other, and highly dependent on the leader for guidance and direction.


* **Storming:** This is the most critical and negative stage, characterized by arguments and barriers; the leader must actively coach the team as processes are established.


* **Norming:** Trust is established, team dynamics settle, and members accept each other's strengths and weaknesses while the leader acts as a facilitator.


* **Performing:** The ideal stage where independent members require minimal supervision, allowing the leader to simply delegate and oversee.


* **Adjourning:** The final stage marking the end of a project, though it is not part of the repeating four-step cycle.



---

## The Five Dysfunctions of a Team

Based on Patrick Lencioni's model, these dysfunctions cascade into one another. Remember the root causes and their remedies:

* **Absence of Trust:** Overcome by building vulnerability-based trust where members are comfortable sharing weaknesses.


* **Fear of Conflict:** Resolved when trusting teams engage in passionate debate to find the best possible truth.


* **Lack of Commitment:** Overcome when teams commit collectively to decisions, even if there was initial disagreement.


* **Avoidance of Accountability:** Addressed by establishing strong peer-to-peer accountability rather than relying solely on the leader.


* **Inattention to Results:** Fixed by focusing on collective team results rather than personal ego, budget, or status.

You will likely see multi-correct questions distinguishing coaching from other management tactics. Keep these distinctions in mind:

## The GROW Coaching Model

This framework is central to the lecture and is heavily tested. Know the acronym and its application:

* **GROW** stands for **G**oal, **R**eality, **O**ptions, and **W**ay forward.


* When using this model, leaders must focus on specific situations and behaviors rather than personal traits, relying heavily on open-ended questions (how/why) rather than closed-ended questions (yes/no).


**Core ideas (memorize):** coaching models, GROW, six-question approach, differences between coaching and feedback.
- GROW = Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward (commitment + obstacles + support).
- Six-question approach: Where are you going? What is going well? What are the barriers? What options do you have? How can I help? What will you do next?
- Coaching ≠ teaching/counselling/feedback. Coaching is mentoring/facilitating discovery; it's future-focused, inquiry-based, and uses open-ended questions.
